Tennis Buddy

24-hour hardware hackathon

We built a tennis training robot in 24 hours, including machining its polycarbonate plates. Tennis Buddy combines a mecanum drivetrain, a flywheel shooter, an adjustable hood, and gravity-fed loading, with onboard cameras and a laptop interface.

I led the robot design and took the shooter and hood from concept to finished hardware. I also helped bring the drivetrain, loading, and controls together before the hackathon ended.

Two 4,000 W BLDC skateboard motors drive the flywheels. Changing their relative speeds gives the ball topspin or backspin; matching them produces a flat shot. A compliant-wheel indexer feeds one ball at a time from the nine-ball magazine.

The hood is the part I'm most proud of. A servo drives it through herringbone gears to reduce backlash and adjust the launch angle repeatably within a tight space. Mecanum wheels provide omnidirectional movement, and an ESP32 handles Wi-Fi control.
